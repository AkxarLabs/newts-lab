# Literature review: the method

How this lab reviews the literature by default. The PI can add to it or replace it (dashboard → Workflow).
Whatever the method, the procedure's contract (SKILL.md) still holds: every query logged, notes as the
only citation source, the verdict vocabulary, and the routing.

## Searching

- Start from the hypothesis's key terms, then widen: synonyms, the methods the first good hits cite,
  and the "cited by" chains of the closest works.
- Combine the logged Semantic Scholar search with web search and fetch, for arXiv and Scholar coverage.
- **Loop until dry:** stop when two consecutive searches surface nothing new and relevant. That is
  typically 8–15 queries.

## Reading

- Read the relevant papers, typically 5–15.
- For each, fetch the actual abstract or paper and write down what it *actually shows* (scale,
  conditions, numbers), not what its title implies. Sloppy notes now become hallucinated citations later.
- Pick the 1–3 load-bearing papers the novelty verdict hinges on. Those get the adversarial critique the
  contract asks for. A "SOTA" that doesn't survive critique changes your baseline obligations.

## The novelty verdict

- Be adversarial with yourself. The default assumption is that someone has done this.
- State the closest prior work and the precise delta between it and this idea.
- For an `incremental` verdict, say what the increment is and whether it is cheap and useful.

## Positioning

- List the baselines the field will demand.
- List the expected metrics and benchmarks.
- List the pitfalls the closest works report; they feed the proposal's risks section.
